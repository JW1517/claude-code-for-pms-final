01 / 06 — Origin Story
Module 1 · Origin Story

Walking in two weeks late

Rook builds coordination software for the protective-response sector. Dispatch works out who's close enough and free enough to help, then gets hold of them. Release 4.2 shipped 12 August — just before this PM arrived, succeeding Priya Raghunathan.

The first move wasn't a meeting, it was reading: everything in 00-rook/ went into one working file, CLAUDE.md, so every later session started already knowing the company.

One thread every later module returns to: on 14 Aug, Marcus Oyelaran asked whether the reweighted routing treats a responder who's declining on purpose the same as one for whom "the world changed under them" — nobody had ever answered it.
Asked
"explain me why Priya said 'August is always soft'? what is the relationship between her answer to the problem?"
Priya's handoff read the spike as likely mostly seasonal — with two overlapping changes (the reweight, the shorter timeout) muddying the signal. She deliberately didn't conclude the release broke anything, and was explicit about one thing:

"Don't let this turn into a conversation about reverting 4.2." The change was earned.
— Priya Raghunathan's handoff note

04 / 06 — X-Ray Vision
Module 4 · X-Ray Vision

The trap, in the code

config.py
WEIGHT_PROXIMITY = 0.60 # was 0.45 WEIGHT_RECENT_ACCEPTANCE = 0.25 # was 0.40 ACCEPTANCE_CREDIT = 0.08 DECLINE_PENALTY = 0.12
history.py
# TODO(wen, 2019): should this ease back toward NEUTRAL_SCORE # on its own after a while? Leaving it as-is for now. def record_declined(responder): _set(responder, recent_acceptance(responder) - DECLINE_PENALTY)
Asked
"Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody… then find me every single thing that puts points back on."
  • record_accepted is the only place that raises a score. record_declined fires identically for a decline or a timeout — no distinction.
  • No passive decay, no handler override — anywhere in routing.py, offer.py, availability.py, config.py. Once a score drops, the only way up is being offered a callout (now weighted mostly on proximity) and accepting it.
  • The TODO(wen, 2019) reads as a genuinely open question left on the shelf, not a decision made for this release.
Answers half of Marcus's question: mechanically, the reweighted logic applies uniformly to anyone with a low score, regardless of why. Doesn't answer whether that was a deliberate 4.2 tradeoff or an unnoticed side effect — still Wen Li's call.
05 / 06 — Super Speed
Module 5 · Super Speed

Building the fix before touching the code

"Before anyone touches the code, I want to see what we'd actually build instead — from the point of view of the person it happens to. Not a setting. Something a handler like Kip would notice, and something a responder who's gone quiet would feel differently about."
— Helen Achebe, Director of Product

The proposal: when a responder's offers-sent has been at or near zero for 2+ consecutive weeks, Dispatch guarantees them one candidate slot on the next matching incident — independent of their history-score rank. A floor on exposure, not a change to how anyone is scored.

  • Does not touch the 0.60 / 0.25 / 0.15 weights or the +0.08 / −0.12 deltas.
  • Does not add decay or a score reset — the TODO(wen, 2019) stays open.
  • Does not let a handler override routing priority in general.
Asked
"Take the brief you just wrote and build me a working prototype, an actual screen I can click through."
